# GDP Growth & Unemployment Analysis

## 📊 Project Overview

This project analyzes GDP growth and unemployment trends across five
South Asian countries: Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nepal, and Sri Lanka
from 2000 to 2025.

The analysis focuses on comparing economic performance, identifying
important trends, and investigating the relationship between GDP growth
and unemployment.

## 🎯 Objectives

- Analyze GDP growth trends over time
- Analyze unemployment trends over time
- Compare economic performance across selected countries
- Identify periods of high and low GDP growth
- Identify periods of high and low unemployment
- Investigate the relationship between GDP growth and unemployment
- Generate meaningful economic insights using data analysis

## 📈 Key Findings

### GDP Growth

India recorded the highest average GDP growth at approximately **6.25%**,
followed by Bangladesh at **5.87%**.

Pakistan recorded the lowest average GDP growth among the five countries
at approximately **3.96%**.

### Pakistan

Pakistan's highest GDP growth during the study period was **7.83% in
2004**.

The lowest GDP growth was **-1.27% in 2020**.

Pakistan experienced negative GDP growth in **2020 and 2023**.

### Unemployment

Pakistan recorded the lowest average unemployment rate among the five
countries at approximately **2.58%**.

Nepal recorded the highest average unemployment rate at approximately
**10.75%**.

Pakistan's highest unemployment rate in the dataset was approximately
**6.34% in 2021**.

### GDP Growth and Unemployment

The correlation between GDP growth and unemployment across all five
countries was approximately **-0.05**, indicating almost no linear
relationship.

For Pakistan, the correlation was approximately **-0.26**, indicating
a weak negative relationship.

These results represent correlation rather than causation.

## 💡 Economic Insight

The analysis shows that higher GDP growth does not automatically
correspond to lower unemployment across the selected countries.

Pakistan provides an interesting example: it recorded the lowest
average GDP growth among the five countries while also recording the
lowest average unemployment rate.

This suggests that GDP growth alone cannot explain differences in
unemployment and that other economic and labor-market factors may also
play an important role.
